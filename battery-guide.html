LiFePO4 vs. AGM batteries: which is right for off-grid solar?

LiFePO4 and AGM batteries can both work in off-grid solar systems, but they differ significantly in usable capacity, cycle life, weight, charging behavior, temperature performance, and upfront cost.

Quick comparison: LiFePO4 vs AGM

The figures below describe common tendencies across consumer and off-grid battery products. Exact specifications vary by manufacturer, model, temperature, charging conditions, and battery management system.

Feature LiFePO4 AGM Lead-Acid
Usable depth of discharge Many products allow substantially deeper routine discharge, often around 80–100% depending on manufacturer specifications and BMS settings. Around 50% is commonly used as a conservative planning assumption when prioritizing battery longevity, although specific products may allow deeper discharge.
Cycle life Commonly rated in the thousands of cycles, but the exact rating and test conditions vary considerably. Typically fewer cycles than LiFePO4, with lifespan strongly affected by depth of discharge, temperature and charging.
Weight Generally provides more usable energy for a given weight. Generally heavier for an equivalent amount of usable energy.
Upfront cost Usually more expensive initially. Usually less expensive to purchase initially.
Cold-weather charging Many LiFePO4 batteries should not be charged below 0°C unless the battery specifically supports low-temperature charging through its BMS or heating system. Generally more tolerant of cold charging than LiFePO4, although low temperatures can reduce available capacity and affect battery performance.
Maintenance Generally low maintenance when properly installed and managed. Generally low maintenance compared with flooded lead-acid, but charging and terminal condition still matter.
These are general planning tendencies, not universal specifications. Always use the manufacturer's datasheet for the actual battery you are buying or installing.

Which battery should you choose?

Neither chemistry is automatically better for every installation. The better choice depends on how frequently you cycle the battery, whether weight matters, your budget, operating temperature, and how long you expect the system to remain in service.

Choose LiFePO4 if...

  • You expect to cycle the battery frequently.
  • You want more usable energy from the same rated capacity.
  • Low weight or portability matters.
  • You plan to keep the system for several years.
  • You want a battery designed for deeper routine discharge.
  • Your battery installation can provide appropriate temperature protection.

Choose AGM if...

  • Your upfront budget is the main constraint.
  • The system will be used occasionally rather than cycled every day.
  • The battery will remain stationary.
  • You already have an AGM-based system.
  • You need a lead-acid solution for a particular existing setup.
  • Cold-weather charging is a concern and lithium low-temperature protection isn't available.

Why LiFePO4 is popular in modern solar systems

Lithium Iron Phosphate, commonly abbreviated as LiFePO4 or LFP, has become increasingly common in portable power stations, RV systems, backup batteries and off-grid solar installations. One of its biggest practical advantages is the amount of energy that can typically be used from its rated capacity.

A battery's rated watt-hours do not necessarily equal the amount of energy you should routinely remove from it. The usable amount depends on the battery's recommended depth of discharge, operating conditions and the manufacturer's specifications.

For planning purposes, a 1,000 Wh AGM battery may be treated as providing roughly 500 Wh of routinely usable energy when limiting regular discharge to around 50%. A 1,000 Wh LiFePO4 battery may provide substantially more usable energy, potentially around 800–1,000 Wh depending on the specific product and its recommended operating limits.

Important: Do not assume that every LiFePO4 battery should be discharged to 100% every cycle. Follow the manufacturer's recommended operating range if you want to maximize battery life.

Cycle life and long-term cost

Battery cycle life describes how many charge and discharge cycles a battery can complete before its capacity falls to a specified level. It does not necessarily mean the battery suddenly stops working at that number.

LiFePO4 batteries are commonly marketed with cycle-life ratings in the thousands, while AGM batteries generally have lower cycle ratings. However, comparing the numbers directly can be misleading unless the test conditions are also compared.

Manufacturers may test batteries at different discharge depths, temperatures, charge rates and end-of-life capacity thresholds. For example, a specification might define end of life as 80% of the original capacity rather than complete failure.

Why cost per cycle matters

AGM can have a lower purchase price, but a frequently cycled system may require replacement sooner. LiFePO4 normally costs more at the beginning but can offer a lower cost per usable cycle when it is cycled regularly over several years.

The correct comparison is therefore not simply: "Which battery is cheaper?" Instead, compare purchase price, usable capacity, expected cycle life, warranty, operating conditions and replacement frequency.

Weight and portability

Battery weight becomes particularly important in portable solar systems, RVs, boats, camping setups and emergency power kits.

LiFePO4 batteries generally provide more usable energy for a given amount of weight than comparable lead-acid batteries. This can make a significant difference when the battery needs to be carried, mounted or transported regularly.

For a stationary off-grid installation, however, weight may matter much less than purchase price, cycle life, available space and charging requirements.

Where AGM can still make sense

AGM batteries are not obsolete. They remain useful in certain applications, especially where the system is inexpensive, stationary or used relatively infrequently.

  • Lower initial budget: AGM can cost less upfront than an equivalent lithium battery.
  • Occasional backup: A lightly cycled emergency system may not benefit as much from lithium's long cycle life.
  • Stationary installation: If the battery never needs to be moved, its greater weight may not be important.
  • Existing lead-acid equipment: Replacing an existing battery with the same chemistry can simplify compatibility with an existing charging system.
  • Cold environments: AGM generally handles cold charging better than unprotected LiFePO4, although cold temperatures still affect lead-acid performance and capacity.

Cold-weather charging: an important LiFePO4 limitation

Temperature is one of the most important differences to understand before installing a LiFePO4 battery in a cold environment.

Many LiFePO4 batteries should not be charged below 0°C because charging lithium cells at sufficiently low temperatures can cause unwanted chemical and physical changes inside the cells.

Modern batteries can address this with a battery management system that blocks charging below a specified temperature, or with integrated heating. The exact operating limits depend on the battery manufacturer.

Never assume that every LiFePO4 battery has the same temperature protection. Check the manufacturer's charging and discharge temperature specifications before installing the battery outdoors or in an unheated space.

What battery chemistry means for system sizing

If you use the solar generator sizing calculator, remember that the rated battery capacity is not necessarily the same as the energy you should plan to use.

A useful first step is to determine your daily energy requirement in watt-hours (Wh). You can then choose a battery capacity based on the chemistry, recommended depth of discharge, inverter losses and the amount of backup time you want.

For example, if a system requires 1,000 Wh of usable energy, choosing a battery with exactly 1,000 Wh of rated capacity may provide little operating margin. A lead-acid system may require considerably more rated capacity than the usable energy target, while a LiFePO4 system can often use a larger percentage of its rated capacity.

Do LiFePO4 batteries need a special charge controller?

A solar charge controller must be compatible with the battery chemistry and the battery manufacturer's charging requirements.

Many modern MPPT controllers provide selectable lithium charging profiles. However, the correct settings vary by battery and manufacturer. Older controllers designed only around lead-acid charging may not provide appropriate settings for LiFePO4.

The controller must also be compatible with the battery bank's voltage, the solar array's voltage and current, and the manufacturer's maximum charging current.

Need to check your solar controller and PV array? Use the MPPT controller sizing calculator as a planning tool, then verify the final configuration against the controller and battery manufacturer's specifications.

Can you mix LiFePO4 and AGM batteries?

As a general rule, avoid connecting LiFePO4 and AGM batteries together in the same battery bank unless the system has been specifically engineered for that configuration.

The two chemistries have different voltage characteristics, charging requirements and discharge behavior. Connecting them directly in parallel can result in uneven current sharing and charging behavior.

If a system needs both chemistries, keeping them as separate battery banks with appropriate charging and protection equipment is generally the safer design approach.

Is LiFePO4 safe?

LiFePO4 is generally regarded as a comparatively stable lithium battery chemistry and has favorable thermal characteristics compared with some other lithium-ion chemistries.

However, chemistry alone does not determine whether a battery installation is safe.

Safety also depends on cell quality, the battery management system, charger and charge controller compatibility, wiring, fusing, installation, mechanical protection and operating temperature.

A properly specified battery and correctly designed electrical system are more important than simply choosing a particular chemistry.

How to compare batteries properly

When comparing two batteries, do not look only at the advertised watt-hour capacity or purchase price. A better comparison includes the following factors:

  1. Rated capacity: Compare the nominal Wh or Ah rating.
  2. Recommended usable capacity: Check the manufacturer's recommended depth of discharge.
  3. Cycle-life test conditions: Check the discharge depth, temperature and end-of-life definition.
  4. Maximum continuous current: Make sure the battery can supply the inverter and other loads.
  5. Charging requirements: Verify the recommended charging voltage and current.
  6. Temperature limits: Check both charging and discharge temperature ranges.
  7. Warranty: Compare the warranty period and conditions.
  8. Physical characteristics: Check dimensions, weight, terminals and installation requirements.
Manufacturer specifications should take priority over generic battery comparisons. Different batteries using the same chemistry can have significantly different performance and operating limits.

Frequently asked questions

Is LiFePO4 better than AGM?

For many frequently cycled solar systems, LiFePO4 offers advantages in usable capacity, weight and cycle life. AGM can still make sense when upfront cost, an existing lead-acid system or occasional use is more important.

How much of an AGM battery can I use?

Around 50% depth of discharge is commonly used as a conservative planning assumption for routine AGM use, but the appropriate limit depends on the specific battery and the manufacturer's cycle-life requirements.

Can I discharge a LiFePO4 battery to 100%?

Some LiFePO4 products are designed to provide most or nearly all of their rated capacity, but that does not mean 100% discharge should automatically be used every cycle. Follow the manufacturer's recommended operating range if maximizing lifespan is important.

How long do LiFePO4 batteries last?

Many LiFePO4 batteries are specified for thousands of cycles, but the actual lifespan depends on the product, depth of discharge, temperature, charge and discharge rates, and how the battery is managed. Cycle ratings should therefore be compared using the manufacturer's stated test conditions.

Do LiFePO4 batteries need a BMS?

A properly designed LiFePO4 battery pack generally requires a battery management system to monitor and protect the cells from conditions such as overcharge, over-discharge and excessive temperature. Integrated BMS functionality is common in consumer LiFePO4 batteries.

Can AGM and LiFePO4 be used in the same solar system?

They can potentially exist in the same overall electrical system, but they generally should not simply be connected together as one battery bank. Separate battery banks and appropriate charging and protection equipment may be required.

Which battery is better for an off-grid cabin?

LiFePO4 is often attractive for a cabin that cycles its battery regularly because of its usable capacity and cycle life. AGM can still be appropriate for a lower-cost system that is used occasionally or where the existing system is already designed around lead-acid batteries.

Which battery is better for a solar generator?

LiFePO4 is now common in portable power stations because it can provide high usable capacity with relatively low weight and long cycle life. The actual specifications still depend on the individual power station.

Sources & further reading

Battery specifications vary substantially between manufacturers. The following types of sources are useful when verifying technical claims or selecting a specific battery:

  • Battery manufacturer datasheets: use the manufacturer's documentation for rated capacity, recommended depth of discharge, cycle life, charging voltage, maximum current and temperature limits.
  • Solar charge-controller documentation: verify lithium charging profiles, voltage limits and maximum charging current before configuring a system.
  • Battery management system documentation: check protection limits and low-temperature charging behavior for LiFePO4 batteries.
  • Applicable electrical codes and installation guidance: follow the requirements that apply to the location and system type when designing permanent installations.
