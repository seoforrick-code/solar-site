How big a solar generator do you actually need to run a fridge and freezer?

Most buying guides quote a single "wattage" number and stop there. That number is nearly useless on its own — sizing a solar generator correctly means working from your daily energy draw in watt-hours, not the sticker wattage of one appliance. Work in watt-hours, not watts

A standard refrigerator draws roughly 100–400 W while its compressor is actually running, and a chest freezer sits in a similar range. But a compressor cycles on and off — it isn't drawing that figure continuously — so the number that determines battery size is the total energy consumed over a full day, measured in watt-hours (Wh).

The formula is simple: Wh = Watts × Hours of actual runtime. A fridge rated at 150 W that runs its compressor for 8 of the 24 hours in a day consumes about 1,200 Wh — not 150 W × 24 h = 3,600 Wh. Manufacturer labels almost never state this, which is why so many people oversize or undersize their setup.

Compressor startup (surge) can briefly spike to 3–5× the running wattage for a fraction of a second. Your generator's continuous rating needs to cover normal draw; its peak/surge rating needs to clear that startup spike, or the unit will trip and shut off.

Sizing in four steps

  1. List every appliance you need to run, along with its running wattage (check the nameplate or a plug-in watt meter — don't rely on marketing copy).
  2. Multiply by realistic daily runtime. For a fridge or freezer, compressor run-time is typically 30–50% of the day depending on ambient temperature and door use.
  3. Add it all up to get total daily Wh, then add a 20–25% buffer for battery inefficiency, temperature loss, and inverter conversion loss.
  4. Match that number to a battery capacity, and separately confirm the generator's continuous and surge wattage cover your highest simultaneous load.

Common questions

Can a small 500 Wh unit run a fridge overnight? For a few hours, yes — but not through a full day-night cycle without recharging. It will typically get you 8–12 hours on a well-insulated compressor fridge before depletion.

Do I need solar panels, or just the battery? The battery alone gives you a fixed runway. Panels let you recharge during daylight so the system can run indefinitely — essential for anything longer than a short outage.

Why does my generator shut off even though it's rated higher than my fridge's wattage? Almost always the startup surge. Check the unit's surge (peak) rating, not just its continuous rating, against your compressor's startup draw.
