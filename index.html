How solar generator sizing works

1. Calculate daily energy

Start with the power consumed by each appliance and estimate how many hours it operates per day.

Energy (Wh) = Power (W) × Runtime (hours)

Add the energy from all appliances to estimate your daily consumption.

2. Add a planning margin

Real systems have conversion losses and operating uncertainty. This calculator uses a 20% planning margin as a simple starting assumption.

That does not mean every system needs exactly 20%. Actual losses vary with the battery, inverter, wiring, temperature, load profile, and equipment efficiency.

3. Size the inverter separately

Battery energy and inverter power are different specifications. A battery can have plenty of Wh while the inverter is still too small to run an appliance.

The inverter must handle the loads that may operate at the same time and must also have adequate surge capability for motor-driven equipment.

4. Check real appliance data

Refrigerator and freezer consumption can vary substantially. Whenever possible, use the manufacturer's energy information or measure the appliance with a suitable plug-in energy meter.

Measured daily Wh is usually more useful than assuming that a compressor runs continuously.

How much power does a refrigerator use?

A refrigerator does not normally draw its maximum running power continuously. Its compressor cycles on and off, while other components such as fans, lights, controls, and heaters may also consume electricity.

Because operating patterns vary, there is no single universal refrigerator wattage or runtime that works for every model.

Best approach: If the refrigerator is already available, measure its energy consumption over 24 hours. A daily Wh measurement can give you a much better starting point for battery sizing than a simple nameplate-wattage × hours calculation.

What about refrigerator startup surge?

Refrigerators and freezers commonly use compressors and other motor-driven equipment. Their electrical demand during startup can temporarily be higher than their normal running power.

However, the amount of startup or inrush power varies by appliance, compressor design, operating conditions, and inverter technology.

Do not blindly use a "3× or 5×" rule. Generic multiplication factors can produce misleading results. When possible, check the appliance documentation or measure startup demand, then compare it with the inverter's specified surge rating.

For a refrigerator or freezer, check both:

  • the inverter's continuous output rating;
  • the inverter's surge/peak rating and duration;
  • the appliance's starting requirements, when available;
  • which other loads may start at the same time.

How much battery capacity do you need?

Battery capacity is normally expressed in watt-hours (Wh) or kilowatt-hours (kWh).

1 kWh = 1,000 Wh

For example, a battery labeled 2,000 Wh contains 2 kWh of nominal stored energy. The amount of energy actually available to your loads can be lower depending on the battery's usable depth of discharge, inverter losses, temperature, battery management system, and manufacturer specifications.

This is why a battery's advertised Wh rating should not automatically be interpreted as the exact amount of energy your appliances will receive.

LiFePO4 note: LiFePO4 batteries often provide a high usable energy fraction, but the manufacturer's specified usable capacity and operating limits should always take priority.

How many solar panels do you need?

Solar array sizing depends on how much energy you need to replace, how many equivalent peak-sun hours are available, system losses, weather, panel orientation, temperature, and the maximum solar input supported by the charge controller or solar generator.

Approx. PV watts = Daily Wh ÷ (Peak Sun Hours × Planning Efficiency)

For example, a 2,000 Wh daily requirement with 4 equivalent peak-sun hours and an assumed 80% system efficiency would give:

2,000 ÷ (4 × 0.80) = 625 W

That does not mean exactly 625 W of panels will always deliver 2,000 Wh. Real solar production changes with weather, season, shading, panel temperature, orientation, and equipment limits.

Solar generator vs fuel generator

Portable solar generator

Battery-based power stations can operate without producing exhaust at the point of use. They are generally quiet and can be recharged from solar panels or another electrical source, depending on the model.

They still require appropriate electrical, thermal, battery, and fire safety practices.

Fuel generator

Gasoline, propane, and other fuel generators can provide substantial continuous power and can refuel quickly.

They produce exhaust and carbon monoxide and must never be operated in enclosed or inadequately ventilated spaces.

Frequently asked questions

How big of a solar generator do I need for a refrigerator?

It depends on the refrigerator's actual daily energy consumption and startup requirements. Instead of choosing solely by refrigerator wattage, determine its approximate daily Wh consumption and then select a battery with enough usable capacity plus an inverter with adequate continuous and surge ratings.

Can a 500 Wh battery run a refrigerator?

Possibly, but runtime depends heavily on the refrigerator's actual energy consumption and the power station's usable capacity and inverter losses. A 500 Wh rating should not be interpreted as a guaranteed number of hours.

How many solar panels do I need for a 2,000 Wh battery?

There is no single answer. You need to consider how much energy must be replaced, available sunlight, system losses, and the maximum solar charging input supported by the battery system.

As an example, 2,000 Wh over 4 peak-sun hours at an assumed 80% planning efficiency gives about 625 W of PV.

Can a solar generator run both a refrigerator and freezer?

Yes, many systems can, but you need to check both the daily energy requirement and the inverter's continuous and surge capabilities. The difficult part can be simultaneous compressor startup rather than the average daily energy alone.

Do I need solar panels to use a solar generator?

No. A portable power station can generally be charged from another electrical source if the manufacturer supports it. Solar panels become important when you want to recharge the system from sunlight or operate more independently from the electrical grid.

Does cold weather affect a battery?

Yes. Battery performance can change with temperature. In particular, many LiFePO4 batteries should not be charged below 0°C unless the battery has a manufacturer-approved low-temperature charging system such as built-in heating or appropriate protection.

Should I choose LiFePO4 or AGM?

LiFePO4 is often attractive for portable and frequently cycled systems because of its energy density and cycle-life characteristics. AGM can still make sense for certain budget, temperature, or existing lead-acid applications.

Read the full LiFePO4 vs AGM battery guide before choosing.

Can I use the calculator as a final electrical design?

No. It is a planning tool. Final system design should account for actual equipment specifications, conductor sizing, protection devices, battery limits, inverter limits, solar input limits, environmental conditions, and applicable electrical requirements.
